There’s a kind of magic in animation that doesn’t quite exist anywhere else. As a kid I grew up watching animated shows practically from the moment I could walk to now in my adult life. 
What pulls me in is not  just the art style or the action—it is the stories. Animation has a way of building entire worlds that feel alive, layered, and real. Shows like Avatar: The Last Airbender didn’t just convey a beautiful overarching story; they constructed unique cultures, histories, and philosophies. Teen Titans wasn’t just about superheroes, it centered itself on the exploration of identity, friendship, and sacrifice in a way that stuck with me long after the screen went black. Even something as lighthearted and silly as Danny Phantom created a protagonist who exists as a living paradox—a Schrödinger's cat suspended between life and death, caught between two worlds and identities.
These weren’t stories told through animation but stories told with animation in mind—crafted to take full advantage of what the medium allows. Animation isn’t just a way to visualize a script; it’s a storytelling tool that can control the very core of the narrative. Animators and artists can warp perception or frame a scene in much different ways than normal film can. My point is that mediums that are used for a creative project are chosen for a reason or design in mind, so when that medium is changed what does that do to the Story? The release of a live action remake of one of my favorite childhood movies How to Train your Dragon spawned the core question of this video. Do remakes add anything to an already established story and specifically Live action ones.
If the answer was determined in the sample size of just How to Train your Dragon then the answer is it does not add any depth once so ever. To be honest, the title of the movie is exactly what it is, a live action remake of the original. It was truthfully one of the most honest and faithful adaptations of a story I have seen in some time. The issue is, I felt close to nothing while watching. The film did not give me any more appreciation for the story or characters and beyond the touching scenes that were already baked into the story it did not particularly move me. That in itself is not indicative of the idea that a remake can add to the story and this is only the one film but the reason why I chose How to Train your Dragon as the center example for this question is for two reasons. Number one, Like I said it's the closest remake to the original I have seen making it the easiest to inspect the inner workings of a story in live action form compared to a Lilo and Stitch which has taken the opposite approach and drastically changed the story. Number Two this is one of the few stories I think that could possibly function as a live action film. We have seen other fantasy genres use live action as the medium like Harry Potter and the end product of those films can be equally immersive, at least from a visual and technical standpoint.

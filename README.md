# TestRepo3
The overwhelming entrapment of loss. The never-ending pounding of grief. Love Death and Robots Spider Rose is a story of anguish and isolation until a shift happens with the introduction of nosey. It hit me soon after finishing the episode that I did not understand the point of the story, specifically what Nosey represented thematically or narratively?
My first thought was to go and read the original short story the episode was based on to understand the theme, which turned out to be a dud. The episode is vastly different from the original, the timeline has been changed, the ending is different, and the overarching tone of anger and revenge is not prevalent. The main difference is really in the characterization of Spider Rose. Her traits that make her feel human are starkly changed from story to episode, to dive into the difference would have to be its own video. 
To summarize my confusion, the story is focused on Rose and her internal struggles over the loss of her husband. The only change is two things, the adoption of Nosey and the eventual revenge against Jade Prime but the revenge happens too late and is used to set up an emotional climax, not as the trigger to start a shift in the theme. Meaning the only major change left is Nosey but what does his presence do to Rose to cause the change and what theme are the creators trying to tell us?
Beginning with the Narrative, on first watch I thought that Nosey was supposed to be the device to establish a twist, that Nosey was a hidden method to eliminate Spider Rose for the Investors to gain the crystal when negotiations seemed to not be possible. The rate that Nosey ate the roaches attached to the timeframe of leaving Nosey in her care seemed like an obvious trap. Reading the short story though I read a quote from Rose that makes me start doubting this idea. Rose says “the Investors were strictly nonaggressive. A pet of theirs would not be dangerous”. While the episode does change aspects of the story, the investors are a recurring entity in Love Death and Robots appearing in the previous season in the episode Swarm, so I don’t believe they have changed the investors stance on intergalactic relations.
	Meaning the trade was done by the creators to have Nosey enter for the purpose of being the method to explore Rose and let her emotion and struggle be the theme. While I think everyone understood that was the purpose, my question is why specifically does Nosey allow Rose that growth? She has pets with her metallic roaches and does not seem interested in companionship; it seems she takes nosey simply because she was free to take for the time being. 
	The first interaction of Rose and Nosey was simple but telling, Nosey is a naïve creature despite its potential advanced age and Rose finds the creatures innocence as charming. Nosey itself takes to Rose without much hesitation and Rose happily accepts the creature’s attention. This is the first time that we see Rose be expressive and to be frank show any human emotion besides the grief and rage she has raging inside of her.
	Rose herself seems trapped in her own past, haunted by nightmares of her husband and crewmates who were slaughtered eight years ago. Her emotional entrapment mirrors the physical isolation of the asteroid web she now calls home—a tangled, desolate space as lifeless and suspended in time as her own mind. She re-lives the event every time she sleeps and every time she daydreams; the only release is a neural sedative she injects herself with. The obsession over her lust for revenge is the sole driving force for her, when the Investors arrive the only item, she desires is a weapon to enact her revenge with, not an item to be able to leave her isolation or start a new life. At this point in the story, it feels as if Rose herself is rather hollow in her long-term desire or her daily life.
	Nosey at first is just an interesting task, a genetic mystery that gives Rose some escapism from her own suffering. While the mystery of why Nosey has so much genetic material does not take long to be solved, it was not its scientific importance that kept Roses interest, it was its behavior. The way it responded to her presence with its playfulness and showed signs of affection and comfort with Rose. It does not take long for Rose to no longer view Nosey as a distraction but the thing in her day she seeks to enjoy. 
	While it may not have been the creators’ explicit intention, Rose gradually begins to act more “human” the longer she cares for and interacts with Nosey — and noticeably less so the more she distances herself from her roaches. When she discovers that Nosey has eaten them, there’s no anger or resistance. Instead, she accepts it with an almost gleeful resignation. This moment subtly confirms a shift: the cold distance she once maintained between herself, and attachment to her own world begins to dissolve. The transition is mirrored physically in her relationship with her "pets” from detached caretaker of expendable insects to an emotionally engaged companion with a creature that responds and seeks her connection. 
	We see as the audience get to see when the depth of Rose’s connection to Nosey becomes undeniable at the moment she begins crying over her past husband. When Nosey jumps into Roses’ arms to comfort her, Rose doesn’t recoil — she accepts it. Rose immediately becomes vulnerable to Nosey, and at that same moment a slow revelation overtakes her. This reaction to open herself to Nosey was an instinctive act. One she has not felt for the past 8 years, and it was a moment of personal discovery that shocked Rose herself. The acknowledgement of the connection that has been building between her and Nosey could not be denied any longer. She has bonded with Nosey. A bond that grounds her to the present and makes her aware of the weight of her own self-imposed isolation. The clarity is apparent when she soothes Nosey but begins soothing herself when she speaks “I’ve been out here too long,” followed by the vulnerable admission, “I still have friends out there”. 
	Nosey is the catalyst used by the Narrative to allow Rose to self-reflect on her own wants and put into perspective how her life choices since the death of her husband have not solved any issue or pain but prolonged it. The conclusion for her self-discovery as it draws nearer is interrupted by an alarm sounding, a sudden arrival of the embodiment of Roses shackles to her past- Jade. The lust for revenge rises filling Roses mind and the taunting of Jade does nothing to clear it. Soon after the station and Jades ship trade artillery fire, Jade is forced to board the station to attempt direct combat with Rose to try and in his own words “finish the job”. The fight was interesting but the thing that kept me interested is that the next scene finally revealed what was the point of Nosey.
